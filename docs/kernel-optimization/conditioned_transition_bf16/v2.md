# conditioned_transition bf16 — v2: inference profiling + optimization-space exploration

Round 2. Goal: profile the now-correct bf16 fused INFERENCE kernel
(`_cond_transition_inference_kernel`, `triton/inference.py`) with ncu and improve it over the
v1 baseline (1.02x@L1024 / 1.10x@L4096 vs compiled torch). **Outcome: the kernel is already at
its practical optimum for this shape** — every structural and tiling variant tried REGRESSES.
No source change ships (all experiments reverted per the "dont hide failed experiments" rule);
this round is the evidence that the v1 baseline is the right thing to keep.

## Environment
- B200 (sm_100a), CUDA 12.8, torch 2.10.0+cu128, pixi `default` env, triton bf16.
- Shapes: A=48, B=1, L∈{1024,4096}, d_hidden=d_pair=128 (atom stream), d_cond=768, n=2 → ND=256,
  K=D=128, DC=768, M=A·L=49152 / 196608. Regime: compile=true, cudagraph=manual, bf16-mixed.
- Shared box: all 8 GPUs run co-resident jobs that cycle busy/idle. Absolute do_bench is noisy;
  the robust signals used here are (a) ncu per-kernel counters (serialized, contention-immune),
  (b) min-of-interleaved-trials do_bench (variants measured back-to-back each round so they see
  identical contention; the min across ~14 rounds catches a clean window), (c) the module-bench
  pytorch-vs-miniworld RATIO (both halves measured in the same run).

## Profile (ncu --set full, L1024, winning config BM=64 BN=64 w4 s2, base clock)
- Duration 104.8 us. **Compute(SM) 32.5%, DRAM 19.9%** — neither hot → not compute- nor BW-bound.
- **Achieved occupancy 11.4%** (theoretical 12.5%). **2 blocks/SM**, co-limited by BOTH
  **Registers 181/thread** (Block-Limit-Registers=2) AND **Dynamic smem 114.7 KB/block**
  (Block-Limit-SharedMem=2). Issued warps/scheduler **0.30** (70% of cycles no eligible warp).
- L1 hit 0.03%, L2 hit 49%. Spills: 0.

### Bottleneck theory
Latency-bound via occupancy starvation. Root cause is structural: one program owns BLOCK_M rows
and does the whole tail, holding **two independent fp32 [BM,D] accumulators simultaneously** —
`out_acc` (built over the ND SwiGLU-squeeze loop) and `scale` (built over the DC cond-gate loop) —
which coexist at the final `y = sigmoid(scale)*out_acc`. Those two accumulators are ~128 of the
181 regs/thread; that plus the pipelined-load smem pins occupancy at 2 blocks/SM (11%). The ops
memory floor is small (~10 us; the 75 MB cond read at L1024 dominates), so the low DRAM% is the
symptom of occupancy starvation, not of a real BW wall.

## Experiments (all measured min-of-14 interleaved trials, clean window; ratios vs v1 baseline)
Baseline fused (BM64/BN64/w4/s2/DC128): **L1024 0.0716 ms, L4096 0.2314 ms** (isolated do_bench).

| experiment | mechanism attempted | L1024 | L4096 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **composed split** (expand+swiglu → h HBM ; squeeze+gate) | 2 lower-reg kernels, higher occ | 0.92x | 0.94x | REGRESS — h[M,256]=25 MB roundtrip > gate-fusion saved |
| **gate split** (expand+swiglu+squeeze → out HBM ; gate) | separate the 2 accumulators; 1 each | 0.90x | 0.92x | REGRESS — out[M,128] roundtrip + 2nd launch > occ gain |
| BLOCK_DC 128→64 | cut DC-loop smem | 0.86x | 0.85x | REGRESS — more DC iters; smem cut doesnt lift occ (regs still cap 2) |
| BLOCK_DC 128→256 | fewer DC iters | 0.69x | 0.67x | REGRESS — smem up, occ down |
| num_stages 2→3 | deeper prefetch | 0.68x | 0.67x | REGRESS — smem up, occ 2→1 |
| BLOCK_N 64→128 | fewer ND iters | 0.59x | 0.58x | REGRESS — bigger tiles, occ down |
| BLOCK_M 64→128 | larger MMA | spill | spill | ptxas 255-reg fail (sm_100a) |

The autotune space in `inference.py` already contains (64,64,w4,s2) and it wins for both L. w8
variants were 2x slower in the config sweep (tiny K=128 MMA doesnt parallelize over warps).

### Why splits lose here but win for `transition` b2b on B200
The MEMORY note "B200 transition b2b fusion loses ~0.45x to split" is about fusing two *large*
GEMMs whose intermediate is huge; there the splits roundtrip is cheap relative to the fusions
register/smem blow-up. Here the fused kernels intermediates are *small* (out[M,128], h[M,256])
and the op is dominated by the single big cond read; the fusion avoids ALL intermediate roundtrips
and the modest occupancy penalty is cheaper than paying even one 12–25 MB roundtrip + extra launch.
So for cond_transition atom inference, **fusion is correctly the winning structure.**

## Decision & next
Keep the v1 fused inference kernel as-is (proven optimal for this shape). No code change ships.
Move the campaigns effort to the real prize: **bf16 TRAINING is fully broken**
(`_b2b_fwd_train_kernel` register-spills at bf16 on sm_100a; the whole train path NaNs). v3 builds
the bf16 train path by reusing the transition-family sm100 cute kernels (`b2b_fwd_sm100`
SwiGLUExpandKernel + `gatebwd_sm100`) + the sigmoid conditioning-gate tail (SPLIT, per the same
memory-bound reasoning — but there the intermediates are large so split is expected to win).
