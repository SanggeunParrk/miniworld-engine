Every knob used to be written at the launch site. Ten of these kernels ran one config on every card and every shape — BLOCK_M=128, BLOCK_N=128, BLOCK_K=16, num_warps=4, num_stages=3, measured once. The pinned-config checklist is one kernel now, and that one fills a buffer with zeros in a single program.

Where GROUP_M is a dash, it is structure, not omission. Two shapes stop it. A LayerNorm epilogue reduces ACROSS the width, so a column tile splits a reduction that has to see the row. And a kernel that chains two GEMMs contracts the FULL width of the first output in the second, so a column tile of the intermediate produces no column of the result. Everything else takes the house set: BLOCK_M1 BLOCK_N BLOCK_K GROUP_M num_warps num_stages.

The seven open ones are two causes, and neither is the kernel being wrong. Five sit outside a band nobody has declared — the same arithmetic run by hand gives identical numbers for all ten gradients, and the error moves 1.28e-02 → 6.43e-02 depending on which config the tuner picked, so a band taken from the lucky one would verify nothing. Two would not launch on an untuned card, where the reader narrows the grid to 24 before Triton sees it and all 24 were over the shared-memory limit; a build offers the full grid and prunes what does not fit.

The gain arrives at length. Across the A5000 sweep the ratio sits near 1.2× from L = 128 to 768 and steps to 1.6–1.7× from L = 1024 up.

The two policies trade at one length. At L = 2048 the save-activation route ran 16.09 ms holding 937 MiB and the recompute route 19.55 ms holding 434 MiB — 21% more time for 54% less memory.
