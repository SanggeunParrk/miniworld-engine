## What changed

`front_c256` now uses one accumulator set. With two sets (chunk c + 1 issued while chunk c is stored), ptxas spilled 104 bytes per
thread at the 168-register cap of a 384-thread block: `setmaxnreg` does not raise the allocation. With one set the other consumer
warpgroup keeps the tensor core busy, so the output is bitwise identical and nothing spills.

| front alone (CUDA graph, ms) | L512 | L1536 |
|---|---|---|
| LN + GEMMs + front_bias (main) | 0.440 | 3.92 |
| front_c256, two sets (round 1) | 0.336 | 2.90 |
| front_c256, one set (round 2) | 0.275-0.289 | 2.43-2.49 |

Before that change, ncu (L1536) showed waits on the weight-chunk barriers (long scoreboard 42 %), tensor 52 % and DRAM 67 %. The
2-CTA multicast of the weight chunks is still level with per-CTA loads (2.46 vs 2.49 ms) and stays off.

## Core (fp16, `triattn_h16_sm90`): measured, nothing kept

ncu at L1536 (head-grouped order, every other exponential pair on the FMA pipe, 1.45 GHz):

- Issue slots 58 % busy, XU 36 %, FMA 43 %, tensor 37 %, DRAM 7.7 GB read per call (was 32.5 GB before the head grouping).
- Stall samples: wait 30 %, selected 26 %, long scoreboard 14 %, dispatch 8 %.
- The kernel is now issue-bound. The polynomial pairs cost 10 instructions each.

Sustained A/B (5 s per variant at the power cap, L1536, against the default of 13.29-14.40 ms):

In the TA_TAU and ring-depth job the node drifted from 13.3 to 15.5 ms across rounds for every variant, so only differences above

## Training (d_pair 256, 8 heads): measured, not changed

Training step (forward + backward, mask, CUDA graph): L384 2.57 ms (both nodes), L768 13.87 / 14.15 ms (starting / ending).

`bwd_q` takes about 2x `bwd_kv` at 4 heads as well, so this is not specific to 8 heads. bwd_q slab sweep (ms):

| H | L | bwd_kv | bwd_q, auto slabs | other slab counts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 384 | 0.36 | 0.60 | best: 4 slabs 0.56 |
| 8 | 768 | 2.58 | 5.05 | all others slower; 1 slab 6.97 |
| 8 | 1024 | 6.0 | 11.5 | all others slower |
| 4 | 768 | 1.29 | 2.43 | all others slower |

What bounds bwd_q (ncu, L768, 8 heads):

- DRAM 60 %: 5.9 GB read and 3.9 GB written per call against ~1.5 GB of operands. The dbias partials (S / QR = 192 TMA reduce-adds per
  element, 3.6 GB) go to DRAM as read-modify-write.
- The reason is the working set. Each running CTA owns a 128-query x S fp32 dbias region (393 KB at L768), and 132 CTAs need
  ~52 MB, more than the L2 holds.
- Determinism forbids sharing a region between CTAs. Splitting by key range removes the dbias problem, but each key chunk then
  needs its own fp32 dq partial (604 MB per chunk at L768 x 8 heads).
- Neither re-split pays as is. Possible next steps: register / DSMEM sums over more pair rows before each reduce-add (QR 8, a 2 x 2
  cluster), or a dbias partial format that keeps fp32 accuracy at half the bytes.
