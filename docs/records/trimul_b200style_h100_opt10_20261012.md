# TriMul H100 opt10 — compact B1 accumulators, 2026-10-12

The D64 compact-sums candidate removes the remaining bidirectional B1 spill, but does **not** establish a consistent complete forward+backward improvement. It remains experimental; the selected opt8 kernel files and dispatch are unchanged. **SOL90 remains unmet.**

This follows the rejected saved-XN experiments in [opt9](trimul_b200style_h100_opt9_20261012.md). Instead of retaining another activation tensor, it shortens the lifetime of duplicate B1 row-sum accumulators. B200-style gate-gradient placement in B7, tensor storage, fusion boundaries and arithmetic order remain as in [opt8](trimul_b200style_h100_opt8_20261012.md).

## Change and compiler evidence

B1 uses an MMA with eight identical columns of ones to accumulate projection and gate row sums. Only two of each thread's four accumulator values are consumed. The candidate retains those two independent values across persistent tiles, reconstructing the four-value MMA fragment immediately before use. It executes the same MMA operations and reduces the same partial buffers in the same order.

PTXAS reports zero stack/spill bytes for candidate B1 with both BF16 and FP32 master weights. Bidirectional B1 still uses 255 registers, but the baseline's 8-byte spill disappears. Single-direction B1 uses 217 registers instead of 222. Shared-memory sizes, B7 source and final reduction source are unchanged. These are compiler improvements, not a claim that the selected implementation was updated.

## Complete-workload measurements

All cells use batch 1, D64, one layer, dropout 0.25, mask probability 0.1, and L128/384/768/1024 in outgoing, incoming and bidirectional modules. Each cell captures both implementations through the official runner with compilation and manual CUDA Graphs, then measures 90 alternating CUDA-event pairs. The observer checks identical input/parameter state and profiles both graphs plus the candidate compiled invocation for actual dispatch and absence of Triton kernels.

| Runner / parameter policy | Cells | Ratios greater than 1 | Opt8 / candidate F+B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Frozen earlier runner, BF16 projections | 12 | 10 | 0.998187–1.005722x |
| Then-current runner, explicit `param_master=False` | 12 | 9 | 0.999256–1.012319x |
| Then-current runner, explicit `param_master=True` | 12 | 7 | 0.996693–1.006167x |

The occasional positive differences, including the 1.23% short incoming BF16 row, are not consistent across cells or repeated regimes. These ratios are sample-median ratios, not confidence intervals. The candidate is not selected based on them. Dropout probability and execution regime match; independent timed graphs are not claimed to use identical random draws.

Job **28177** uses runner SHA256 `9c414e84c20b74a3c94f16d6928066a0910e0ce306ea8d4d15dbc43463f9181a`. Job **28185** repeats the candidate with then-current runner SHA256 `9b724e9e76e36c75cad6b23bc5ce83fcb79503b7117fdfc7ab3d90f0572c2490`, explicitly selecting each master policy.

That second runner was subsequently found to construct MiniWorld projection weights in BF16 before converting them back to FP32 under `param_master=True`. Thus the last table row uses **BF16-rounded values stored as FP32 for both native control and candidate**. Their states match exactly, but the row is not a comparison against a vendor initialized with unrounded FP32 values. It must not be presented as that deployment condition. A separate matched-master audit repairs this runner initialization; none of this report's measurements silently substitutes the repaired runner.

## NCU

The same-process L384 captures in job 28177 profile native full training invocations with kernel replay. They are diagnostics rather than the whole-module timings above.

| Kernel | Opt8 µs | Candidate µs | Candidate SM throughput | Candidate DRAM throughput |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Outgoing B1 | 49.824 | 48.608 | 37.43% | 50.51% |
| Bidirectional B1 | 64.480 | 63.616 | 35.29% | 56.29% |

Bidirectional B1 local-load/store sectors fall from 73,708/73,716 to zero. DRAM reads remain essentially unchanged: about 78.02/78.01 MB before/after for bidirectional B1 and 59.11 MB for outgoing B1. Eliminating these local accesses therefore does not remove a similarly sized HBM transfer. Unmodified B7 durations also vary between captures; those differences are not attributed to this B1 edit.

The observed utilization remains below both SOL90 and the requested 70–80% range. Removing the spill alone does not solve the remaining scheduling and memory costs, and this experiment does not prove a fusion limit.

## Validation and evidence

- Output and every gradient are bitwise equal to opt8 in 12 cases: L128/L384, three directions and both master-weight types.
- The expanded 15-case suite passes on each source snapshot, covering all gradients, L1024, changed input/weight/mask/dropout/dy graph replay, zero-mask behavior and outstanding forward ownership.
- The current-source candidate passes all 48 strict oracle cases across eight lengths, three directions and both master-weight types. Output relative error stays below 0.006 and every gradient below 0.01; thresholds are unchanged.
- Memcheck, racecheck and synccheck at L256 cover all three directions, persistent tile reuse and changed graph values. All nine direction/tool combinations have zero errors or hazards.
- The six opt8 baseline files are checked against their selected manifest. The initial runner-hash mismatch is retained in `baseline-verification.log` rather than ignored or rewritten.

Work ran on node02 through Slurm `h100/cssb/normal_h100`, with one H100 per GPU job, explicit memory requests and isolated source snapshots. No experimental kernel was installed and no commit or push occurred.

Evidence is frozen under `runs/benchmarks-h100/benchmarks/modules/triangle_multiplication/results/h100/b200style_opt10_20261012/`: **304 files listed in `SHA256.json`**, including 36 paired cells, two NCU reports with eight kernel captures, compiler diagnostics, strict numerical cases, sanitizer logs, generators, source variants, official runner artifacts and the original runner-verification failure. `results/d64-compactsum-summary.json` separates the three measurement regimes.
