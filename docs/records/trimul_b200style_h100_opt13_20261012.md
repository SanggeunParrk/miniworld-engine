# TriMul H100 opt13 — D256 saved outputs through TMA, 2026-10-12

D256 training K3 now saves projection and gate values through TMA while retaining its original statistics calculation. Compared with [opt12](trimul_b200style_h100_opt12_20261012.md), all **48 training conditions improve in both graph-capture orders**, giving **96/96 paired wins** and **1.007903–1.038686x complete forward+backward speedup**. Fresh FP32-master comparisons beat cuEquivariance 0.12.0 in **24/24 training shapes, by 1.161248–2.772289x**. The inference algorithm is unchanged; fresh D256 inference beats the pristine Anthropic payload in **16/16 supported shapes, by 1.015538–1.060140x**.

Four files are installed behind the existing `MINIWORLD_TRIMUL_H100_B200STYLE=1` path. Its default remains zero. **SOL90 and the requested 70–80% utilization range remain unmet.** The vendor advantages mostly predate this edit; the approximately 2.6–2.8% geometric-mean training improvement is the incremental kernel result.

## Change and scope

The original D256 K3 stores rounded projection and gate values with per-thread global stores. The new `k3scalar_tmasave.cu` retains packed projection values in dead accumulator registers and retains the existing packed gate values. After the output TMA store finishes reading shared memory, it reuses that same tile to store projection, then gate, through TMA. Store-read waits, per-warpgroup barriers and the final store-completion wait protect these lifetimes. No extra saved tensor, global allocation or kernel launch is introduced.

Gate-first GEMM order, folded input/output LayerNorm, scalar statistics, reductions, rounding points, tensor layouts and B200 fusion boundaries are unchanged. Producer/consumer register budgets remain 40/232. Both D256 hidden widths compile with 168 reported registers, zero stack bytes and no local load/store instructions. The saved tensors remain necessary backward inputs; this change does not eliminate their HBM traffic.

The selected files are `h100_b200style.py`, the new `h100_b200style_scalar_tmasave.py`, its CUDA source, and `tests/gpu/sm90/test_trimul_h100_b200style_gpu.py`. Only D256 with `save=True` selects the new CUDA entry. Inference, D384 and D512 retain their preceding algorithms. Opaque operation names are distinct so the frozen baseline and candidate can coexist during paired measurement. A source audit checks 2,300 existing source files: the host dispatch file is the sole existing source changed in the candidate snapshot. The original inference helper and K3 source are byte-identical.

## Correctness and sanitizer scope

The prototype passes 67 GPU tests; the selected four-file version passes **73**, including six explicit output-kernel dispatch checks for D256/D384/D512 in inference and training. Across all 48 D256 length/direction/master conditions, the output and eight saved forward tensors are bitwise identical to opt12. Scaled inference agrees with training output, and inference agrees bitwise with the old inference implementation. Every input/parameter gradient on both native sides satisfies the unchanged FP32-oracle limit of 0.01; output uses 0.006. In the official full grid and vendor comparison, maximum native output/gradient relative errors are **0.00363426 / 0.00663696**.

An initial probe incorrectly required bitwise equality of every gradient and stopped on the projection-weight gradient. The old kernel alone, repeated eight times on the same input, reproduces that discrepancy in all seven adjacent comparisons: relative differences range from 7.30e-6 to 3.64e-5. Its existing backward contains atomic reductions. The original failure, control experiment and script remain in the evidence. The final checks require exact forward state and the original independent FP32 accuracy limits; they do not claim bitwise gradient equivalence or loosen those limits.

At D256/L256, six changed-value graphs cover all three directions in inference and training. Each changes inputs, weights/affines, masks and applicable dropout scales/dy over three trials, including the zero-mask case. Whole-module **unfiltered memcheck and synccheck pass with zero errors**. Full-module racecheck restricted to K3 also passes with zero hazards. A separate **unfiltered standalone K3 racecheck** passes four H256/H512 × L128/L256 cases, including persistent tile reuse, three changed trials and bitwise checks of output, projection, gate, mean and rstd.

These are scoped racecheck results. An earlier unfiltered full-module racecheck on the matrix-statistics candidate was explicitly stopped after more than seven minutes of CPU-bound instrumentation without completing its first case. Its partial log is retained; it is not reported as a pass.

## NCU

Same-process baseline/candidate training K3 profiles at L384 use kernel replay. Whole-module paired timings above determine selection.

| Direction | Opt12 K3 µs | Opt13 K3 µs | Opt13 SM throughput | Opt13 DRAM throughput |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Outgoing | 342.624 | 279.776 | 24.63% | 41.86% |
| Bidirectional | 376.640 | 320.640 | 25.29% | 43.77% |

Global LSU store sectors fall from 9,474,048 to 36,864 in each profile. This counter excludes the replacement TMA writes: it is not a claim that total HBM stores disappear. Outgoing DRAM reads change 199.60→187.41 MB and writes 211.80→205.18 MB. Shared-load wavefronts fall 5,207,655→4,179,895, while shared-store wavefronts rise 770,771→2,360,369. Registers remain 168. These counters and the speedup do not establish a hard limit on further fusion optimization.

## Rejected alternatives

A D512-style projection-first K3 port was tested at D256/D384. Restricting gate-loop unrolling removes the D256 spills; all 67 GPU tests pass. Nevertheless, all eight inference pairs lose, with ratios 0.960775–0.994936x. Only two of eight training pairs improve, spanning 0.996936–1.003200x. NCU inference K3 also regresses: D256 127.616→143.648 µs and D384 222.176→237.248 µs. Compilation without spills was insufficient for selection.

A gate-first matrix-load/quad-statistics variant improves six of eight inference pilots but only three of eight training pilots. Adding TMA saves produces convincing training gains, and its first 96-cell grid wins every cell. Reversing capture order exposes five inference losses, down to 0.992246x. That variant also wins its 16 Anthropic and 24 cuEquivariance comparisons, but its inference improvement over opt12 is not consistent. It is not installed. The selected version instead retains the original scalar statistics and inference algorithm. A D384 matrix-statistics TMA-save compile has 16 stack bytes and ten local instructions; it is not GPU-qualified or selected.

One reverse run stops because its single graph-replay profile omits forward K3. The original 30 completed rows and error are retained. A separate problematic-shape pilot and the complete repeat use three profiling replays, still requiring the exact K3 and zero Triton. Timed event pairs and CUDA source are unchanged by this observer correction. Profiling repeats are not additional timing samples.
