# transition_fused_sm100 — round v11: quad-layout LayerNorm-backward epilogue (measured, not adopted)

- Date: 2026-09-25
- Kernels unchanged from v9; patch `records/experiments/bwd_quad_layout_epilogue.patch`.

## 1. Finding: `tcgen05.ld.16x256b` is the wgmma C fragment
`ldshape.cu` shows thread t of a warp receiving rows (t / 4) and (t / 4 + 8) of a 16-lane half and columns 2 (t % 4), + 1 of each
8-column group; `.16x128b` gives one column per row. So the sm_90a kernel's epilogue layout (a quad holds a row) is available on
Blackwell straight from tensor memory.

## 2. Variant
The DX LayerNorm backward rewritten in that layout: d_xn read 16 rows at a time (`16x256b.x16`), row sums by two quad shuffles (the
sm_90a order), each thread's 32 columns of dgamma / dbeta partials kept in registers across all tiles and reduced once at the end
(no per-tile reduce-scatter), x / dy / dx as conflict-free 4-byte shared accesses. Correct (same errors), spills 196 → 84 bytes, and
in the trace the epilogue became idle-waiting (it keeps up easily).

## 3. Why it is not adopted
Back-to-back launches (graph or eager) average 445-525 µs: nsys shows most launches at ~250 µs but occasional launches of ~2.9 ms
(`b2b_probe.py`). v9 has no such outliers (215-245 µs per launch). The stall is not a missed mbarrier wake-up (a non-suspending
spin-wait build shows the same); the cause is not found. And even at 250 µs the variant does not beat v9: the DX chunk remains
~3300-3500 clk, bound by the weight-slot / accumulator chains of v10, not by the epilogue. The epilogue speed-up is only useful
together with a DX layout whose buffer reuse depends on it (v10 §3).

## 4. Addendum — forward with three `[a|b]` accumulators (`src/tfwd3.cu`, kept, not default)
h written in place over the first 32 columns of its own `[a|b]` buffer (freed by the squeeze), so TMEM holds three `[a|b]`
buffers and the expand can run two chunks ahead. Correct (out vs contract 8.9e-5, bit-reproducible). L384: 58.5 / 61.9 µs
(inference / training build) vs tfwd2 58.5 / 61.7; L768: 241.3 / 219.6 vs 242.0 / 221.1 — neutral. (Measured after the B200's
other tenant released the GPUs at 21:20; contended runs between 19:36 and 21:20 were discarded.)
