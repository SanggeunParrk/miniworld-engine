- Date: 2026-09-25
- Target kernels: `transition_fwd_sm100`, `transition_bwd_sm100`
- Baseline: v1 (`ce84e328`)

Same as v1 (B200, 1965 MHz, CUDA 13.1, torch 2.10 cu130). ncu still unavailable (`ERR_NVGPUCTRPERM`); evidence from the clock64

`./build.sh tfwd && ./build.sh tbwd`, `python bench_fwd.py --length {128,384,768}`, `python bench_bwd.py --length {128,384,768}`,

## 3. Pre-change profile (v1)
Forward chunk period 2222 clk against 768 clk of tensor work; SwiGLU warps waited 416 clk/chunk on `[a|b]`; MMA issue period equal
to the SwiGLU period (`profiles/v1/trace_fwd.txt`).

## 4. Bottleneck theory and how it evolved (all measured, forward L384)
The v1 hypothesis (weight-ring refill latency on the critical path) was **wrong**: splitting the ring and adding a second SwiGLU
warpgroup moved 86.7 → 82.5 µs only, and an ablation that never reloads weights after chunk 1 (`-DABL_NOW`) did not change the
time. Stripping all SwiGLU math, TMEM loads and LayerNorm (`-DABL_NOMATH -DABL_NOLN -DABL_NOSWLD`) still left **70 µs**, so the
floor was in the MMA pipeline itself. Stamping the MMA warp showed it spent ~1150 clk *issuing* the 8 expand MMAs of a chunk while
all its barrier waits were ~70 clk. The SASS showed why: each `tcgen05.mma` was wrapped in an `ELECT / R2UR.BROADCAST / BRA.U.ANY`
waterfall loop, because the MMA loop ran on lane 0 only (divergent code), so ptxas could not keep the descriptors in uniform
registers.

Micro-benchmarks (`profiles/v2/mma_bench.txt`) fixed the hardware numbers used from here on:
- M128 N128 K16 runs at 64 clk (8188 FLOP/clk/SM, peak) SS and TS; there is a floor of **~43-47 clk per tcgen05.mma** whatever N
  is (N = 16/32 → 45 clk; two issuing warps do not beat it), so N = 64 products run at 67 % of peak.
- A `tcgen05.commit` costs ~70 clk of pipe time when interleaved with MMAs (977 vs 768 clk per chunk pattern).

## 5. Changes (one theme: keep the tensor pipe fed)
Forward:
1. MMA warp runs converged; one `elect.sync` lane issues; descriptors built once per chunk and advanced by constants (no waterfall).
2. Two issuing warps: warp 1 issues all expands, warp 2 all squeezes; each waits only on its own operands, so one warp's waits never
   leave the pipe without queued work (previous single-thread order put expand(c+2) behind squeeze(c), i.e. behind SwiGLU(c)).
3. Weight ring split into `[Wa_j; Wb_j]` (freed by the expand) and `Ws_j` (freed by the squeeze); one producer thread polls both
   rings non-blocking. Commit barriers merged (`ex_done`, `sq_done`, each waited by two parties).
4. Two SwiGLU warpgroups in ping-pong over the two `[a|b]` buffers (512 threads; setmaxnreg 56 / 152).
Backward: items 1 and 2 applied to both roles (DW: warp 1 dh + `[a|b]`, warp 2 weight gradients; DX: warp 1 dh + `[a|b]`, warp 2
`d_xn`). A second gate warpgroup + setmaxnreg was tried in the backward and **reverted**: ptxas then spilled 4.6 KB across the whole
kernel (two role functions with different register plans) and the backward went to 987 µs. The backward stays at 384 threads.
DW replica sweep re-run: R = 8/9/10/11/12/13 → 332/294/**265**/309/335/402 µs → R = 10.

Tried and reverted: releasing the output accumulator before the epilogue math (128 extra live registers → more spills, L768 worse).

## 6. Validation
Unchanged numerics (same rounding points, same kit sigmoid): forward out vs contract 8.9e-5 (L128), backward dx 1.6e-4,
dW* 4.3e-4, dgamma / dbeta 1.4-1.6e-4 (L384); all bit-reproducible run to run.

## 8. Where the forward stands (`profiles/v2/trace_fwd.txt`)
Chunk period ~1400 clk. The SwiGLU warpgroups are now the busy unit: 1836 clk of TMEM-load + math per chunk per warpgroup, with
two warpgroups sharing each SMSP — close to the MUFU floor (kit sigmoid = 2 MUFU per element → 1024 clk per chunk per SMSP).
Tile boundaries add ~1000-1500 clk (the next tile's first squeeze waits for the previous epilogue to read the accumulator).

## 9. Next ideas (ranked)
1. Backward trace (same instrumentation) — the DX role runs at ~4.7 k clk per chunk vs 1408 clk of tensor work, the DW role at
   ~4.5 k clk per tile vs 1792; both are far below the forward's efficiency.
2. Forward: move part of `ex2` to a polynomial on the FMA pipe (FA4-style) with accuracy equal to `ex2.approx`, to get the SwiGLU
   under the 768 clk tensor time; then double-buffer the output accumulator to remove the tile-boundary bubble.
3. Backward: merge dh (N = 64, 67 % efficient) and `[a|b]` into one N = 192 product.
