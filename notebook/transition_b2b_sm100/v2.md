## Why "fused loses" is NOT the conclusion (the H100 lesson)
The H100 hand-CUDA fused b2b **started at v0 = 0.825× of Triton — a LOSS — and lost for 11 rounds**,
only winning at **v11 (1.20×, vectorized STG.128 epilogue)**, then v12–v14 → **1.29×** (bank-conflict
swizzle, WG-local barriers, gamma/β register-load). The win came from **targeted micro-optimizations on
a 2-warpgroup hand kernel**, NOT a warp-specialized-collective rewrite. Our B200 fused kernels are at the
v0 stage; the raw .cu (0.05×) is even more naive than H100 v0 (single WG, scalar scatter, zero overlap),
so it has abundant low-hanging fruit.

Physical ceiling note: B200 HBM ≈ 8 TB/s, so the h[M,ND] round-trip fusion removes is ~38 µs of the
split's 110 µs (d=128, L=384) → fused ceiling ≈ 1.5× over split IF pipelined as well as the split. (On
H100, ~2.4× lower BW made the round-trip costlier, hence its 1.9× fusion win.) So the B200 prize is
smaller than H100's but real.

## Campaign plan (raw .cu, ncu-driven, one commit per round; mirror the H100 recipe)
1. **Vectorized epilogue store** (H100 v11's breakthrough): the scalar per-element swizzled h-scatter →
   STG.128 / vectorized r2s. Biggest easy win.
2. **Overlap** expand/silu/squeeze: BN=64 so `2·(64+64)+D ≤ 512` TMEM cols → `num_acc_stage=2`; software-
   pipeline (issue expand(c+1) before blocking on squeeze(c)); double-buffer h smem.
3. **2-warpgroup cooperative** (256 threads) like the H100 kernel, + bank-conflict swizzle + WG-local
   NamedBarriers + gamma/β/param register-load (H100 v12–v14).
Correctness gate every round: cos ≥ 0.999 (d=128 & d=256). Compare vs split each round.
