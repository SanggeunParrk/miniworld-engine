# layernorm_bwd CUDA (standalone) -- sm100 (B200) -- v1

Target kernel: `layer_norm_bwd_main_kernel<scalar_t, MAX_K, TX_BYTES>` in
`src/miniworld_engine/kernels/layernorm/cuda/layer_norm_cuda_kernel.cu`
(warp-per-row + register column-partials + persistent grid + contiguous-per-thread
uint2/uint4 vector loads). Dispatched by `layernorm/compile_native.py` for bf16, 128<=N<=512.

## Goal
Bring the hand-CUDA LN backward -- previously benchmarked only on H100 (sm_90, 1.23-1.48x
vs triton) -- onto B200 (sm_100), confirm the "uniform coalesced / vectorized load"
principle still wins, and re-tune the H100 knobs (WAVES, TX_BYTES, MAX_K) for sm100.

## Environment
- GPU: NVIDIA B200 (sm_100, 148 SM, HBM3e ~8 TB/s), CUDA 12.8, torch 2.10.0+cu128.
- Build: nvcc -O3 --use_fast_math, gencode sm_80/sm_90/sm_100 + compute_100 PTX
  (commit 1835802 added the sm_100 gencode that made this kernel loadable on B200).
- Bench: benchmarks/runners/bench.py kernel=layernorm_bwd, bf16, compiled + manual
  CUDA graph, triton.testing.do_bench(graph.replay) median. Grad cosine on dx vs the
  pure-torch fp32 LN backward. Rows: pytorch(pure), triton_atomic/partial/persistent, cuda.
- Shapes: N(d) in {128,256,384,512}, M = L*L for L in {384,768,1024}.

## Baseline (pre-change): the principle transfers to sm100
CUDA (original H100-tuned kernel) beats the best triton path on B200 across the whole
d/L grid -- and by MORE than H100's 1.23-1.48x at d>=256. Speedup vs best triton (bench
manual-cudagraph median, ms):

| d | L=384 | L=768 | L=1024 |
|---|-------|-------|--------|
|128| 1.08x | 1.08x | 1.11x |
|256| 1.61x | 1.45x | 1.40x |
|384| 1.76x | 1.76x | 1.76x |
|512| 1.67x | 1.77x | 1.78x |

d=128 is the weak point (~1.1x); d>=384 is the strongest (~1.7-1.8x).

## Profiling / bottleneck analysis (ncu, base clock, main kernel only)
| shape | kernel | regs | DRAM% | SM% | occ% | waves/SM | ld sec/req | st sec/req |
|-------|--------|------|-------|-----|------|----------|-----------|-----------|
|d128 L1024|<bf16,4,8> uint2 | 56 | 26.9 | 62.9 | 44.8 | 0.89 | 5.67 | 8.28 |
|d512 L1024|<bf16,16,16> uint4| 121| 39.8 | 64.9 | 23.9 | 1.00 | 14.33 | 16.56 |

Findings:
- Vectorization is IDEAL on sm100. Store sectors/request hits the theoretical max for
  the chosen transaction (uint2 = 8 sec, uint4 = 16 sec) exactly. Load sec/req is a bit
  lower only because W is re-read from the same N addresses every row (L1-cached, averages
  the ratio down). The coalesced vector-load design works exactly as intended on Blackwell.
- Both shapes are SM-throughput-bound (SM ~63-65%), NOT DRAM-bound (27-40%). This is a
  warp-per-row op: the residual cost is issue/latency, not bandwidth.
- d=128: under-occupied (0.89 waves, 45% occ) -- tiny rows (4 cols/lane) make the two warp
  reductions dominate. Structural to warp-per-row at small N.
- d=512: register-limited occupancy (121 regs -> 4 blocks/SM -> ~25%). Structural to the
  register-column-partial design (MAX_K fp32 accumulators per lane).

## Tuning experiment 1 -- WAVES (persistent grid size): H100 value already optimal
Swept LNBWD_WAVES in {2,4,6,8,12,16} (env, read once per process) at L in {384,1024}:
- d=128: WAVES=8 best at both L (L1024: 8=247us, 16=262, 6=292, 4=390). Matches shipped default.
- d=256/384/512: WAVES=4 best at both L (higher waves grow the partial-buffer + reduce cost
  faster than they add main-kernel occupancy). Matches shipped default.

Conclusion: the H100-measured heuristic choose_bwd_grid = (N<=128)?8:4 is ALSO the
sm100 optimum. No change. (Negative result -- WAVES does not transfer-break.)

TX_BYTES (uint4 when N%256==0 else uint2) and MAX_K (=N/32, capped) were confirmed correct
by ncu (kernel template tags <bf16,4,8>, <bf16,16,16>) and left unchanged -- the store
sec/req proves both are landing full-width coalesced transactions.

## Tuning experiment 2 -- cache w*dy across the two passes (KEPT, the v1 change)
Hypothesis: the kernel is SM-issue-bound, and it loads W twice per row (once in the
sum-reduction pass, once in the dx pass) and recomputes wdy = W*dy in both. Caching
wdy in registers after the reduction pass and reusing it in the dx pass removes
MAX_K global W loads + MAX_K multiplies per row. Math/precision unchanged.

Change: added `float wdy_vals[MAX_K]`, store wdy in the sum pass, drop the second
W vector load + the W*dy recompute in the dx pass.

Expected risk: +MAX_K registers could drop occupancy at d=512 (already reg-limited).
Measured: no occupancy loss (d=128 regs 56->55; d=512 regs 121->128 = still exactly
4 blocks/SM). SM% dropped (fewer redundant instructions), DRAM% rose slightly (same work,
less time).

Result (manual-cudagraph median, cuda ms, before -> after, and speedup vs best triton):

| shape | before | after | vs best triton (after) |
|-------|--------|-------|------------------------|
|d128 L768 | 0.1472 | 0.1432 | 1.12x |
|d256 L768 | 0.2784 | 0.2744 | 1.47x |
|d384 L768 | 0.3788 | 0.3624 | 1.84x |
|d512 L768 | 0.3991 | 0.3910 | 1.81x |
|d128 L1024| 0.2436 | 0.2374 | 1.14x |
|d256 L1024| 0.4852 | 0.4771 | 1.43x |
|d384 L1024| 0.6573 | 0.6266 | 1.84x |
|d512 L1024| 0.6889 | 0.6757 | 1.82x |

Net +1.7% to +4.7% on every shape (biggest at d=384), no regression anywhere.

## Validation
- Grad cosine (dx) vs pure-torch fp32 backward: >= 0.999998 on every shape (CSV grad_cosine).
  Smoke also checks dw/db cos (>= 0.99999).
- CUDA graph replay vs eager: worst_cos = 0.9999989 (bf16, effectively bit-exact; the
  <1e-6 is cosine-metric rounding on ~1e8-element vectors -- the kernel has no atomics and
  a deterministic reduction, so it is graph-safe).

## Forward (context, not the target)
Standalone LN forward on B200 (L=768): triton fwd ~0.057-0.207ms vs pytorch ~0.75-1.07ms
(~13x). The forward CUDA kernel is a naive block-per-row (not vectorized) and is not wired
into the forward dispatch; the vectorization principle lives in the backward.

## Conclusion
On sm100 the coalesced-vector-load hand-CUDA LN backward WINS over triton across the whole
bf16 128<=N<=512 regime (1.08-1.84x), and wins by more than it did on H100 at d>=256. The
H100 tuning (WAVES=8/4, TX uint4/uint2, MAX_K=N/32) is already the sm100 optimum -- no knob
needed changing. The only impl-level headroom found was the redundant-W-load elimination
(v1, +1.7-4.7%). The residual bottleneck is SM-issue/occupancy, which is structural to the
warp-per-row + register-column-partial algorithm (out of scope per the no-algorithm-change rule).
