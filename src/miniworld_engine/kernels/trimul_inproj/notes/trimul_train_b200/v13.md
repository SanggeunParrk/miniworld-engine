# trimul_train_b200 — round v13 (front GEMM shape-specialized config)

- Date: 2026-07-05  ·  Branch `perf/trimul-train-b200`  ·  GPU NVIDIA B200 sm_100 (cap 10.0)
- torch 2.10.0+cu128, quack 0.3.11, nvidia-cutlass-dsl 4.4.2. bf16 in / fp32 acc / bf16 out.
- Target: the front GEMM in `front_train_sm100.py:89`
  (`gemm_act(A=x_flat, B=b_lr, activation=None, store_preact=False, postact_out=preact_view)`).
  Front = one non-gated GEMM `x_flat(M=L², K=D=128) @ b_lr(128, 4D=512) -> preact`, bf16 in / fp32
  acc, **m-major postact store** (postact strides `(1, M)`), activation=None.
- Goal: re-tune this GEMM **per shape/dtype** and wire the best config, honestly reporting whether
  quack's autotuner was leaving anything on the table.

## Step 1 — what config is ACTUALLY selected today (probe)
The round premise ("quack's `@autotune(key=["activation","dynamic_scheduler"])` is shape-blind, so
one bucket serves all shapes") turned out to be **incorrect for quack 0.3.11**: the autotuner
(`autotuner.py __call__`) auto-appends **each tensor's shape, bucketed stride, and dtype** to the
cache key. Verified with `QUACK_PRINT_AUTOTUNING=1` — three distinct cache keys, one per L. So the
front GEMM is already tuned per shape. quack picks (tile256×512, cluster(2,1), pingpong=False):

| L | M=L² | quack pick |
|---|---|---|
| 384 | 147 456 | swap_ab=True,  dyn_persistent=True |
| 768 | 589 824 | swap_ab=True,  dyn_persistent=True |
| 1024 | 1 048 576 | swap_ab=False, **dyn_persistent=True** |

Note: `autotune(..., cache_results=True)` is the real default (docstring wrongly says False), so the
pick is benchmarked with a short/noisy `do_bench(warmup=5, rep=25)` and persisted to
`~/.quack/cache`. The selection is thus dependent on that fragile disk cache and on bench noise.

## Step 2 — exhaustive per-shape sweep (ground truth)
Swept **all 148 valid sm100 configs** (`get_all_configs()` filtered to `device_capacity==10 &
!use_tma_gather`) for the EXACT front problem at each L, timed by CUDA-graph
`do_bench(warmup=25, rep=100)`, each verified `cos ≥ 0.9999` vs fp32 `x@b_lr` (all 148 passed, every L).

| L | swept best config | best GEMM | quack pick GEMM | GEMM speedup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 384 | tile256×512 cl(2,1) swap_ab=T dyn=T | 40.83 µs | 40.83 µs | **1.000× (== pick; ceiling)** |
| 768 | tile256×512 cl(2,1) swap_ab=T dyn=T | 141.09 µs | 141.09 µs | **1.000× (== pick; ceiling)** |
| 1024 | tile256×512 cl(2,1) swap_ab=F **dyn=F** | 260.03 µs | 269.22 µs | **1.035×** |

Verdict: for L=384/768 the swept optimum **equals** quack's own pick → we are at quack's
config-space **ceiling**. For L=1024 the swept optimum beats the pick by 3.5% — not a tuning gap in
the search space but **short-bench noise**: quack's warmup=5/rep=25 cannot separate 260 vs 269 µs, so
it kept `dyn_persistent=True`; the rigorous CUDA-graph bench picks `dyn_persistent=False`.

## Step 3 — wiring
`front_train_sm100.py`: added `_front_gemm_config(M, device)` returning the swept-best `GemmConfig`
for `M ∈ {384², 768², 1024²}` on sm100/sm110, else `None`. The call site injects it via
`gemm_act_tuned.fn(..., config=cfg)` — identical idiom to quack's own
`gemm_act_out(tuned=False) = partial(gemm_act_tuned.fn, config=None)`, i.e. bypass `@autotune` and
supply an explicit config. **quack site-packages untouched.** Non-sm100 / unknown-M fall back to the
autotuned `gemm_act`. CUDA-graph safe (no benchmarking at call time). Precision unchanged
(bf16 in / fp32 acc) — config selection only.

Value of the wiring: (a) captures the L=1024 3.5% GEMM win; (b) **determinism** — the config no
longer depends on `~/.quack/cache` (which currently pins the 3.5%-worse config for L=1024) or on
autotuner bench noise; (c) removes the cold-start 148-config autotune on first call.

## Gate — performance (CUDA-graph replay, sanctioned; ms, single-dir D=128 B=1 bf16)
| L | v13 graph | v12 graph | cueq graph | v13/cueq |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 384 | 0.6513 | 0.651 | 1.1108 | 1.706× |
| 768 | 2.2292 | 2.218 | 3.9642 | 1.778× |
| 1024 | 4.0242 | 4.036 | 6.9970 | 1.739× |

End-to-end is **unchanged within noise** vs v12 — expected: the front GEMM is ~6% of a training step
(40.8/141/260 µs of 651/2218/4036 µs), and the only per-shape gain (L=1024, 3.5%) is ~0.2% of the
step, below do_bench median jitter. v13 stays comfortably ahead of cuequiv (1.71–1.78×).

## Verdict & next step
- **quack's front-GEMM config space is at its ceiling for our shapes** (best == pick for L=384/768;
  L=1024 within 3.5%, closed by pinning). Re-tuning yields no measurable end-to-end win — quack was
  already well tuned, not under-tuned. The wiring is kept for determinism + the small L=1024 gain.
- The real headroom is **not config selection** but the kernel itself: ncu still shows Tensor ~25%,
  occupancy ~11% (MMA starved by the single K=128 tile / short main loop). Beating this requires a
  **shape-specialized custom front kernel** (e.g. split-K or a fused GEMM+GLU epilogue tailored to
  K=128, N=512, m-major store), not another quack config. That is the next round.
